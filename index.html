Chapter 1

Development, research, theories
Multidimensional development
Development occurs across physical, cognitive, and social-emotional domains.
Tap card to reveal
A preschooler becomes better at balancing on one foot, learns to count higher, and becomes more comfortable playing cooperatively with classmates.
Developmental cascade
A change in one area can produce changes in another area.
Tap card to reveal
As a child’s language improves, they can explain what they want more clearly, which may reduce frustration during interactions with others.
Plasticity
Development can change in response to experience.
Tap card to reveal
A child who initially has difficulty reading becomes much more skilled after receiving targeted instruction over time.
Multicontextual development
Development is influenced by multiple settings and circumstances.
Tap card to reveal
A child’s experience is shaped by expectations at home, relationships with classmates, community resources, and cultural beliefs.
Multidirectional development
Development can involve gains in some areas while other abilities remain stable or decline.
Tap card to reveal
A teenager becomes faster at solving abstract problems while becoming less interested in activities they enjoyed as a younger child.
Continuous development
Development occurs gradually over time.
Tap card to reveal
A child’s handwriting slowly becomes more controlled and precise across several years.
Discontinuous development
Development occurs through qualitatively different stages.
Tap card to reveal
A theorist argues that children’s reasoning changes in distinct ways rather than simply becoming gradually faster or more accurate.
Active development
Children help shape their own developmental experiences.
Tap card to reveal
A curious child repeatedly takes apart old toys to figure out how they work and learns through that exploration.
Observation
Researchers watch and record behavior without manipulating a variable.
Tap card to reveal
A researcher sits in a preschool classroom and records how often children enter ongoing group play.
Experiment
Researchers manipulate an independent variable and measure a dependent variable.
Independent variable = changed · Dependent variable = measured outcome
Tap card to reveal
Researchers randomly assign children to two different memory-training activities and compare their later recall.
Case study
One person or situation is studied in great depth.
Major limitation: limited generalizability
Tap card to reveal
A developmental psychologist closely studies one child who experienced an extremely unusual form of early sensory deprivation.
Longitudinal research
The same people are measured repeatedly over time.
Tap card to reveal
Researchers begin studying a group of 3-year-olds and return to assess those same children every two years.
Cross-sectional research
Different groups are compared at one point in time.
Tap card to reveal
Researchers compare a group of 4-year-olds, 8-year-olds, and 12-year-olds during the same semester.
Sequential research
Several age groups are studied and then followed over time.
Tap card to reveal
Researchers begin with several age groups and then continue following each group over time.
Microsystem
People and settings the child interacts with directly.
Tap card to reveal
A child’s daily interactions with a soccer coach.
Mesosystem
Connections between parts of the child’s microsystem.
Tap card to reveal
A child’s after-school program begins coordinating regularly with the child’s family.
Exosystem
Settings that affect the child indirectly.
Tap card to reveal
A parent’s employer changes work schedules, which changes when the parent is available at home.
Macrosystem
Broader cultural values, beliefs, laws, and social expectations.
Tap card to reveal
Cultural beliefs about how much independence children should have influence parenting practices.
Chronosystem
The role of time, transitions, and historical circumstances in development.
Tap card to reveal
A child grows up during a major period of technological or social change that alters everyday childhood experiences.
Positive reinforcement
Something desirable is added after a behavior, making that behavior more likely.
Tap card to reveal
A child receives a sticker whenever they put away their materials, and they begin cleaning up more consistently.

Chapter 2

Heredity, prenatal development
Passive genotype–environment correlation
Parents provide both genes and an environment related to those genes.
Tap card to reveal
Parents who are naturally strong readers pass on characteristics related to language ability and also fill their home with books.
Evocative genotype–environment correlation
A child’s characteristics draw out responses from other people.
Tap card to reveal
A highly persistent child causes adults to give them increasingly challenging activities.
Active genotype–environment correlation
A child actively selects environments that fit their tendencies.
Tap card to reveal
A child who enjoys physical activity chooses to spend lunch playing basketball rather than sitting indoors.
Genotype–environment interaction
The same environment can have different effects depending on genetic susceptibility.
Tap card to reveal
Two children experience the same highly demanding environment, but it produces much greater anxiety in the child with greater biological susceptibility.
Epigenetics
Environmental conditions can affect gene expression without changing the DNA sequence itself.
Tap card to reveal
An environmental experience changes the activity of genes without changing the underlying DNA sequence itself.
Mitosis
Cell division that produces two genetically identical body cells.
Tap card to reveal
As a child grows taller, body cells reproduce to create additional cells.
Meiosis
Cell division that produces sperm or egg cells with half the usual chromosome number.
Tap card to reveal
Before conception occurs, reproductive cells are produced with only half of the usual chromosome number.
Genotype
The genetic information a person inherits.
Tap card to reveal
Someone carries genetic information associated with a trait, whether or not that trait is visibly expressed.
Phenotype
The characteristics that are actually expressed or observable.
Tap card to reveal
The visible or measurable form of an inherited characteristic is the phenotype.
Heterozygous
Having two different versions of a gene.
Tap card to reveal
A person inherits one dominant allele and one recessive allele for the same characteristic.
Down syndrome / Trisomy 21
A chromosomal condition caused by an extra chromosome 21.
Tap card to reveal
A karyogram shows three copies of chromosome 21.
Germinal period
Conception through implantation, roughly the first two weeks.
Tap card to reveal
Several days after fertilization, a rapidly dividing group of cells is approaching the uterus.
Embryonic period
The period when major body structures begin to form.
Tap card to reveal
At five weeks, the basic foundations of several major organs are being established.
Fetal period
Week 9 through birth, with continued growth and maturation of major structures.
Tap card to reveal
At six months, many basic structures already exist and are continuing to grow and mature.
Age of viability
The point at which survival outside the uterus first becomes possible, around 24 weeks.
Tap card to reveal
Doctors are discussing whether a very early fetus has reached the point at which survival outside the uterus may be possible with intensive medical care.
Teratogens
Environmental factors that can interfere with prenatal development.
Risk depends on timing, amount, multiple exposures, and susceptibility.
Tap card to reveal
Two prenatal exposures occur at different points in pregnancy, and the earlier exposure affects a structure that was forming at that particular time.
Ultrasound
Uses sound waves to examine the fetus.
Tap card to reveal
A clinician uses sound waves to check fetal growth and position.
Amniocentesis
A small sample of amniotic fluid is collected for diagnostic testing.
Tap card to reveal
A clinician collects a small sample of the fluid surrounding the fetus for diagnostic testing.
Chorionic villus sampling
A small sample of placental tissue is collected for genetic testing.
Tap card to reveal
A clinician obtains a small sample of placental tissue for genetic analysis.

Chapter 3

Birth, newborn assessment
First stage of labor
The cervix dilates as contractions become stronger and closer together.
Tap card to reveal
Contractions are becoming more frequent while the cervix progresses from 4 centimeters to 8 centimeters dilated.
Second stage of labor
The baby moves through the birth canal and is delivered.
Tap card to reveal
The cervix is fully dilated and pushing begins as the infant moves toward delivery.
Third stage of labor
The placenta and remaining fetal membranes are delivered.
Tap card to reveal
Several minutes after the infant is born, the remaining fetal membranes and placenta are expelled.
Braxton-Hicks contractions
Irregular contractions that do not progress like true labor.
Tap card to reveal
Late in pregnancy, contractions occur irregularly and then disappear rather than steadily increasing.
True labor
Contractions become progressively longer, stronger, and closer together.
Tap card to reveal
Contractions become progressively longer, stronger, and closer together over time.
Induction
Labor is medically started rather than beginning on its own.
Tap card to reveal
Pregnancy has progressed well beyond the expected due date, so medication is used to begin labor.
Cesarean section
The baby is surgically delivered through the mother’s abdomen.
Tap card to reveal
During labor, clinicians determine that the infant cannot safely be delivered vaginally and perform surgery to deliver the baby.
Lamaze
Controlled breathing, relaxation, a focal point, and support-person coaching.
Tap card to reveal
Expectant parents practice patterned breathing and focusing attention while a partner learns how to provide support.
Bradley Method
Natural childbirth preparation emphasizing relaxation and partner coaching.
Tap card to reveal
A couple prepares for an unmedicated birth with substantial emphasis on relaxation and partner coaching.
Hypnobirthing
Preparation aimed at reducing fear and tension through relaxation, breathing, support, and education.
Tap card to reveal
Preparation focuses on reducing fear and tension through relaxation, breathing, and education.
Alexander Technique
Emphasizes posture, movement, and conscious control of physical responses.
Tap card to reveal
Preparation emphasizes posture, movement, and changing habitual physical responses to discomfort.
Apgar assessment
A quick assessment of the newborn’s immediate physical condition, repeated shortly after birth.
Heart rate · respiration · muscle tone · reflex response · color
Tap card to reveal
Immediately after delivery, a clinician quickly evaluates several signs of how well an infant is making the transition to life outside the uterus, then repeats the evaluation a few minutes later.
NBAS
A more detailed assessment of newborn motor behavior, muscle tone, and stress response.
Tap card to reveal
After the immediate post-birth period, a clinician conducts a more detailed assessment of how a newborn moves and responds to stimulation and stress.
Preterm
Born before 37 weeks of gestation.
Tap card to reveal
An infant is delivered several weeks before the pregnancy has reached 37 weeks.
Low birth weight
Birth weight below 5 pounds 8 ounces.
Tap card to reveal
A newborn reaches full term but weighs considerably less than 5 pounds 8 ounces.
Small-for-date
Birth weight is lower than expected for the infant’s gestational age.
Tap card to reveal
A baby reaches 39 weeks but weighs much less than would normally be expected for that gestational age.
Postmature
A pregnancy continuing to about 42 weeks or later.
Tap card to reveal
A pregnancy continues beyond 42 weeks.
Anoxia
A temporary lack of oxygen to the brain.
Tap card to reveal
A complication temporarily interrupts the infant’s oxygen supply around the time of delivery.
